# Architecture of the Dragon Tower (竜の塔, Ryū no Tō) in the Tower Dungeon manga

Research date: 2026-10-10. This extends `docs/research/tower-dungeon-since-vol6.md`, sections 4, 5, 7 and 11. It does not repeat what is already there.

## 0. Method and limits

- **Tags used.**
  - [primary] means publisher copy or an official title. I found no Nihei interview about the tower's design (Natalie, Kodansha and English searches), and no museum or exhibition material. The only author-side fact is that he worked as a construction site supervisor before becoming a manga artist, which reviewers cite as the source of his spatial sense.
  - [critic] means a named review outlet or a long-form blog.
  - [fan] means reader comments, Fandom, TV Tropes or review aggregators.
  - [inferred] means my own reading of the above.
- **Attestation grades** for each space: **A** is two or more independent sources that agree. **B** is one reviewer or the publisher. **C** is wiki or fan only. **D** is a title or inference only.
- **Pages that could not be read.**
  - Fandom returned 402 to WebFetch and 403 to curl. The archive route is blocked in this environment.
  - I reused the Fandom page texts cached earlier in this session's scratchpad, which are copies of the wiki. These are always graded C.
  - Reddit is blocked at the search tool, so ResetEra stands in for forum talk.
  - StoryGraph and Anime-Planet returned 403. Their content appears here only through search-result summaries, which I flag.
  - Booklive, Bookmeter and Booklog pages mostly gave only partial or empty review lists.
- **Source hygiene.**
  - [mintotaku.blogspot.com](https://mintotaku.blogspot.com/2026/04/tower-dungeon-manga-review-niheis.html) closely echoes the ANN vol 2-4 wording, so I count them as one source.
  - nba1.hateblo.jp and gasemedaka.com read as thin or auto-generated. gasemedaka is used once and marked low-trust.
  - The manga's own pages could not be inspected. Every spatial claim below is second-hand.

## 1. How reviewers frame the tower as a whole

- **The tower is the protagonist.**
  - [critic] [Anime UK News](https://animeuknews.net/2025/11/tower-dungeon-volumes-1-2-review/) says the series "puts its focus into the titular dungeon itself". It contrasts this with Nihei's other works, which focus on massive machine-built societies. It also says "atmosphere certainly takes precedence".
  - [critic] The Japanese guide [shinentai](https://note.com/shinentaicom/n/n8da8f2d6d4c0) says 本作の主役は「竜の塔」そのものといっていい ("the star of this work is the Dragon Tower itself").
  - [critic] ANN says new, weirder things appear "with each successively higher floor".
- **Publisher framing.**
  - [primary] [PRH and Vertical copy](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/778939/tower-dungeon-1-by-tsutomu-nihei/): Nihei is "master of the mysterious architectural monolith" bringing his "architectural eye" to fantasy. The setting is "a crumbling world of malformed creatures, wounded soldiers, and labyrinthine darkness".
  - [primary] [Kodansha JP vol 1](https://www.kodansha.co.jp/comic/products/0000386075): 天から降りてきた巨大な建造物「竜の塔」 ("a giant structure that descended from the sky"). Comic DAYS opens with 天高く聳える巨大な"竜の塔" ("the colossal Dragon Tower soaring to the heavens").
  - [primary] Vol 7 copy calls the inhabitants 禍なる塔の住人 ("the tower's calamitous residents").
- **Author background.**
  - [critic] [Urbane Turtle](https://urbaneturtle.com/2025/08/04/manga-review-tower-dungeon-basks-in-the-beautiful-and-grotesque/): "The star of the book is the setting and architecture, which is intricately and meticulously illustrated."
  - [critic] The shinentai guide credits "the sense of space gained from architectural work" and says the structure drawing is unmatched: 背景の構造物描写は他の追随を許さない.
  - [fan] [Booklive and Honto readers](https://honto.jp/ebook/pd-review_0633397586.html) say 相変わらずの変態建築登場で安心できました ("the usual perverse architecture shows up, reassuring") and スケール感、空間の広がりがケタ違いだ ("scale and spatial breadth are on another level").
- **Material palette.**
  - [critic] ANN vol 1: buildings "look like they've been hewn straight from rock, or hastily cobbled together with ill-fitting blocks", not "metal and carbon fibers".
  - [critic] [Goodreads reviewer Sol](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/214519414-1-tower-dungeon-1) (via search summary): it is nice to see Nihei work "exclusively in brick stone and wood".
  - [critic] ANN vols 2-4: "Everything he draws looks suitably old, weathered, and pleasingly analog."
  - [fan] A Honto reader finds a nostalgic mood unlike ordinary dungeon manga: ノスタルジーな雰囲気.
  - [critic] Anime Atelier and the shinentai guide describe a decayed, feudal, post-apocalyptic feel rather than classic medieval fantasy.

## 2. Catalogue of distinct spaces

Each entry gives where it is, its visual attributes, the evidence and a grade.

### 2.1 Exterior and approach

**S1. The tower seen from outside (floating, mountain-dwarfing shaft). Grade A.**
- It reads as a vertical monolith that dwarfs mountains and sits at or above ground level.
  - [critic] ANN vol 1: "a massive pillar of indeterminate size, levitating vertically, high above the mountains it dwarfs."
  - [critic] Manga Book Club: "so enormous it appears to have fallen from the sky" and "suspended just above the ground".
  - [critic] Urbane Turtle: "wide long shots of the eponymous tower, stretching far into the clouds."
  - [critic] Anime Atelier: the colossal size is revealed over three full pages, "almost biblical, a labyrinth that pierces the heavens".
  - [critic] Library Comic: "the mountainous size of the Dragon Tower that Nihei's art creates".
  - [fan] Idiot's Delight (Hatena): the tower is 山より巨大 ("bigger than a mountain") and ちょっと浮いている ("slightly floating"), a trait the writer likes. The same post asks whether the entrance bridge was built by people or by chance, and how it floats.
- **Conflict.** One Bookmeter reader says it hangs 天空から垂れ下がっている ("down from the sky"), while the publisher says it "descended". The ch.1 wiki summary says it was summoned from the sky. Treat "floating just above the ground, arrived from the sky" as the safe reading, and "hanging from the sky" as one reader's impression.
- Size figures:
  - [critic] ANN vols 2-4 says "three kilometers wide and 1800 floors tall".
  - [fan] A reader comment on mancome says a tower map appeared and cites 18 km, 3 km and 1800 floors.
  - [fan] TV Tropes says about 15 km and 10 m per floor.

**S2. Fortress and entrance bridge. Grade C.**
- [fan] The wiki says a ruined fortress is joined to the tower by a giant bridge that is the only foot entrance, with a harbour on the nearby river.
- [fan] Idiot's Delight independently notes the entrance bridge.
- Reviewers add only "crumbling barracks" and "sprawling landscapes" (Urbane Turtle).

**S3. Airspace ring (dragons circling). Grade B.**
- [fan] TV Tropes and the wiki describe dragons mauling Countess Yeoa when she tries to fly up in ch.6. [critic] AUKN says "Her plans to simply fly to the top ... were thwarted."
- Architecturally this means the tower has no usable outside.

### 2.2 Entry and lower floors

**S4. Floor 1: columned ruins with water. Grade C.**
- [fan] Wiki: "numerous columns", "in ruins", apparently safe but full of undead. Ch.1: Yuva falls into a trench full of water just inside the entrance.
- [critic] Urbane Turtle's "endless staircases" and "massive columns" is the only reviewer echo, and it is not floor-specific.

**S5. Staircases: narrow, crumbling, sheer. Grade A.**
- [critic] Anime Atelier: "Narrow staircases and sheer drops are transformed into dizzying panels that impart a constant sense of claustrophobia".
- [critic] ANN vols 2-4: "impossibly high, crumbling spiral staircases".
- [fan] A Hatena blogger finds the tower shape fresh for a dungeon story: narrow footing at height and fall risk, unlike underground dungeons.
- [fan] TV Tropes' running-gag entry: ledges and walls keep giving way under Yuva, so he finds new areas by accident.
- [fan] Wiki, ch.6: the party leaves the spiral stair at a broken stretch and takes a detour. Yuva falls off a ledge in an ambush.

**S6. Wooden stairs on the outer wall. Grade C.**
- [fan] The wiki's ch.2 summary says the squad climbs and notes wooden staircases built on the tower's outer wall "by people in the past".
- No review mentions it. A Bookmeter reader and ResetEra post #16 do say there are sections open to the outside, even though the tower is a tower.
- The detail is plausible but single-sourced.

**S7. Floor 14 loop and waterway. Grade C.**
- [fan] Wiki ch.3. A room inscribed "14" has identical siblings, so the party walks in circles; Eliquo's map was wrong because the rooms are separate but look identical.
- Yuva breaks a wall and drops into a room with a large channel of running water and a staircase beyond. A monster patrols the channel back and forth. Two gatekeepers guard another stair.
- Reviewers mention none of this. The Anime UK News vol 1-2 review does not cover floor 14.

**S8. Hidden crawlspace warren of the Ruins-Dwellers (遺構人, Ikō-hito). Grade B.**
- [critic] AUKN vol 2: the primeval inhabitants provide a stairwell that leads to level 80.
- [fan] Wiki ch.4: a room reached through a hole in the wall and a crawlspace, where insects are cooked with salt. Yuva breaks a wall again to find it.
- [fan] A Hatena blogger (gasemedaka, low-trust) lists them as people who live in the tower and have a different ecology.

**S9. The hidden spiral staircase, floor 1 to floor 80. Grade A.**
- [critic] AUKN (vol 2) and [fan] wiki and TV Tropes ("hidden central staircase", "dungeon bypass").
- [fan] Wiki chapter titles name ch.8 as "The secret spiral staircase". That is a fan translation of a Japanese title I did not confirm.
- The wiki says the top section was damaged until the Excavators' Union repaired it.
- TV Tropes says it is open only to certain floors.
- **No visual description of the stair itself was found in any review.** Its look (width, whether it runs through the central shaft) is unattested.

**S10. Floor 20: potable water and Dragon Spores. Grade C.**
- [fan] Wiki and ch.1 summary: clean water and the mushroom on or near a corpse.
- Architecture-wise this is a resource nook, not a described room.

### 2.3 Mid floors

**S11. Floor 50 great hall and the Chymus' arena. Grade B.**
- [fan] TV Tropes: Chymus mucus stuck the Royal Guard "on the various columns in the area". The wiki says Yuva jumped from a bridge onto the slime and climbed to a high place first.
- So the hall is columned, with an elevated bridge or perch and a stair guarded by a gatekeeper.
- [fan] Wiki also says giant slimes take up "massive halls" just to roam.
- [critic] Idiot's Delight (Hatena) says the writer especially likes the depiction of "slime" and "floor 80" in vol 1.

**S12. Gatekeeper thresholds (門衛, Mon'ei) and royal-blood locks. Grade A for existence, B for lock mechanics.**
- Gatekeepers sit at stairs and gates:
  - [critic] AUKN: "towering sentries".
  - [critic] Library Comic: "a door guardian".
  - [primary] Official ch.20 title, 巨甲の門番と大地を拒む力 ("the giant armoured gatekeepers and the power to refuse the ground").
  - [fan] Wiki: one before the floor 50 stair, two at the floor 14 stair, and pairs below floor 70.
  - [fan] Yuva's line in ch.20 (wiki quote): they are "almost like old windmills walking the halls".
  - [fan] Wiki: they lock doors even against royal blood.
- Royal-blood doors:
  - [critic] [Anime Fire vol 5](https://animefire.com/2026/08/07/tower-dungeon-vol-5-official-review/) says "a door that only someone of royal blood can unlock".
  - [fan] Wiki: touching a special lock with the hands.
- Look: black shining armour, white metal face-mask. Reviewers add "gothic armor with expressionless white faces" (Urbane Turtle) and "heavily armored knights" (japaneselit.net).
- Safeguard emblem: [fan] the wiki and TV Tropes note it. [fan] A Booklive reader (しま) notes Safeguard and Biomega echoes. [primary-adjacent] The Manga Taisho nomination says メガストラクチャー、東亜重工製フォントなど、弐瓶先生流のスターシステムは健在 ("megastructures and Toha Heavy Industries fonts, Nihei's star system, are alive and well").

**S13. Floor 59-60 Great Hall (basilisk hall). Grade A.**
- [critic] [AUKN vols 3-4](https://animeuknews.net/2026/04/tower-dungeon-volumes-3-and-4-review/): the trio reach a "vast Great Hall" on level 59 with a basilisk; the Excavators say they met the heroes on the 60th floor.
- [fan] Wiki: basilisks petrify many victims in the main hall; Karash petrified and killed at floor 60.
- The review names the hall but gives no pillar, ceiling or layout.

**S14. Floor 100 well. Grade D.**
- [fan] Wiki: a well where Ignelia was seen. A fan translation of the ch.10 title is "The well on the 100th floor". Nothing visual.

### 2.4 Floor 80 and the structural framework

**S15. Floor 80: the single huge hall. Grade B for existence, C for geometry.**
- [critic] AUKN: the dragon's remains lie "on the 80th level".
- [fan] Wiki ch.2: they reach a main hall at the 80th floor, "a gigantic room that stretches horizontally like a cross-section of the whole tower"; as they approach a staircase the half-bodied dragon wakes.
- [fan] Wiki page: the floor is 1.86 miles in diameter and 219 yards high (about 3 km by 200 m). This conflicts with 10 m floors, so it spans many levels or is wrong.
- A reviewer's phrase "horizon line" does not appear anywhere. The nearest attested concept is the wiki's "stretches horizontally like a cross-section". Treat the horizon idea as [inferred].
- [fan] Wiki: after the army was destroyed the floor became the undead army's home. The stair at the far side of the hall is the one the dragon guards.

**S16. The 12 main pillars, the ring dividers and the cross-section. Grade C.**
- [fan] Wiki: 12 main pillars run to the top and divide the tower into sections. Circular structures divide it into three bands from the centre, giving 36 sections in cross-section. A cross-section diagram was handed to Eliquo by royal archaeologists, with a gallery image on the wiki.
- [fan] A mancome commenter confirms a tower map appears, with 18 km, 3 km and 1800 floors, and asks how anyone would climb it.
- No critic describes the 12 pillars. They cannot be graded above C.

**S17. Reference pillars with floor number and coordinates. Grade C.**
- [fan] Wiki: each floor has a pillar with the number in large letters and coordinates below. The ch.6 summary has Eliquo explaining this after talking to architects.
- Only wiki.

**S18. Pillar maze (柱の大迷宮, Hashira no Dai-meikyū), ch.28. Grade D.**
- [primary] Official ch.28 title: 柱の大迷宮と三角の進軍 ("the great pillar maze and the triangular march"). The wiki's only gloss concerns Funapa's gathering ability.
- No review, blog or summary describes the maze. Everything about its look is [inferred]: repeated pillars as walls, probably tied to the 12 main pillars or the floor-number pillars.

### 2.5 Cross-cutting qualities

**S19. Cathedral and vault. Grade B.**
- [critic] Manga Book Club: "vaulted stone ceilings, crumbling masonry, and massive columns".
- [critic] japaneselit.net: "vaulted ceilings and crumbling stone walls"; "labyrinthine passageways".
- [critic] ANN vols 2-4: "wide open Great Halls supported by colossal pillars".
- The word "cathedral" is not used by any source. It is [inferred].

**S20. Distortion: "inside larger than outside". Grade C for the literal claim, B for perspective distortion.**
- The literal claim comes from one blog: Manga Book Club says "the tower feels larger on the inside than it does from the outside".
- The Wikipedia plot section mentions "impossible geometry" and "twisted architecture" with no citation. I could not confirm either in a reviewer.
- What is attested is a perspective effect:
  - [critic] Anime Atelier: "complex, overhead shots and puzzling angles that twist and warp the reader's perception."
  - [critic] ANN vol 1: "bizarre yet effective angles that truly conjure an air of terrifying verticality".
  - [critic] StoryGraph reviewer (via search summary): the walls feel "close and cramped" as architecture bleeds over panels, while landscape shots feel "suffocatingly open".
  - [critic] Goodreads and StoryGraph readers also say some panels are hard to parse (Rod Brown, Joseph; a Booklive reader says コマに入ってる絵がまじでわかりづらい).
- The looping identical rooms on floor 14 (S7) are the only in-fiction spatial paradox I found, and only in the wiki.

**S21. Organic growth. Grade D.**
- [critic] Wikipedia paraphrases ANN reviewer Kevin Cormack as praising "organic architecture". I did not find that phrase in the ANN text I could read, so it stays unconfirmed.
- Organic is attested for the creatures, not the building: "disturbingly organic beasts" in Otaku USA, "gooey, and organic" in ANN. Treat "organic growth" as unsupported for the tower (consistent with section 3 of the repo doc).

**S22. Wordless landscape panels. Grade A.**
- [critic] AIPT: "numerous pages featuring wordless panels that showcase the eerie atmosphere of the dungeons".
- [critic] Shinentai: scenes where several pages of wordless panels convey only strange space.
- [critic] Urbane Turtle: some pages carry no dialogue.
- [fan] Manga Taisho nominators praise one-panel dialogue-free scenery they could look at forever: セリフが無い風景だけの一コマがずっと見てられるぐらい美しく.

## 3. Double-page spreads and panel composition

- **Where spreads fall.**
  - [critic] Urbane Turtle: travel scenes open into double-page spreads, and the tower's entry is shown in "multiple full-page vertical views".
  - [critic] Anime Atelier: three full pages reveal the tower's size.
  - [critic] [inkstone](https://note.com/inkstone/n/ndf9b539cfc28): the spreads are seamless because there is no gutter (のど), so they read as pure pictures. The writer points to a spread in vol 4.
  - [critic] Goodreads (Shells, via search summary): "the 1 panel full landscape illustration" as a remnant of BLAME!'s style.
- **Big-pane reveals.**
  - [fan] A Booklive vol 5 reader likes pages where the dungeon space suddenly looks wide: ダンジョンの空間がいきなりバコンって広く見えるようなページ.
  - [fan] Another reader (Honto) says occasional big panels are beautiful (時々入る大きいコマがきれい).
  - [fan] A Booklive vol 1 reader (ぶれいく) says Nihei fits pictures larger than the panel naturally, which is rare.
  - [critic, low-trust site] gasemedaka (vol 5) says spreads show the interior and the maze's breadth.
- **Pacing.**
  - [critic] The 24hryabookblog reviewer says the action is "decompressed, stretching the tension".
  - [fan] Pages cut from long wordless sequences to a single reveal panel, going by the reports above. I did not see the pages.
- **Contrast technique.**
  - [critic] Urbane Turtle: creatures "popping out in stark white against the inky shadows", with "heavy blacks of the cavernous interiors".
  - [critic] 24hryabookblog: a dragon "in charcoal silhouette".
  - [fan] Goodreads (Sol): some vol 1 pages are "more than 50% black".

## 4. "White Nihei" as used here

- **The term.**
  - [critic] [shangrila69](https://www.shangrila69.com/tower-dungeon-review/) is the only source I found using it: かつての『BLAME!』や『BIOMEGA』が緻密な描き込みによる「黒の弐瓶」だとしたら、本作は線が整理された「白の弐瓶」 ("if BLAME! and BIOMEGA were the dense 'black Nihei', this is a line-organised 'white Nihei'").
  - It adds that cutting extra lines makes depth and bigness easier to read, and calls the open drawing a "new frontier". It does not use the words "negative space" or "contrast".
  - A web search for 白弐瓶, 黒弐瓶 and "white Nihei" found no other source, so this is one blogger's coinage.
- **Corroborating descriptions of the same look, without the label.**
  - [critic] inkstone: 細部を省いて線を選び取った絵 ("a picture with detail omitted and lines selected"), strong black-and-white contrast, a graphics-like quality. The writer is reminded of Matsumoto Taiyo and says single pictures can stand alone, for example "Aridellia bathing".
  - [fan] A Booklive reader (みー) praises 白と黒の潔さ ("the clean decisiveness of black and white").
  - [critic] ANN: "deceptively simple yet confident" linework with "stunning vistas".
  - [critic] ResetEra: "less scratchy and more streamlined", with raw but confident single strokes and heavy black areas.
  - [critic] Manga Book Club: moves away from "heavy black inks and rough cross-hatching" to "a simpler, looser approach".
- **Complication.** The simple line does not mean a pale page. Interiors keep heavy blacks (Urbane Turtle), a tension the shangrila69 piece does not address.
- **Negative reactions** (relevant for legibility):
  - [fan] Several Booklive readers say the art is rough, a draft, or resembles Fujimoto Tatsuki and Chainsaw Man.
  - [critic] AIPT: "doesn't reach the hyper-detail of Blame!".
  - [critic] ANN vol 1: the simpler style also gives more varied, distinguishable characters.

## 5. How it differs from BLAME!

- **Material.**
  - [critic] ANN: Tower Dungeon has "no sci-fi element so far", and its architecture is stone, not metal and carbon fibre.
  - [critic] japaneselit.net contrasts vaulted stone with "the claustrophobic cable-choked interior of a spaceship".
- **Scale.**
  - [critic] ANN vols 2-4: BLAME!'s city is "even more mind-bogglingly enormous". The Tower is a bounded Big Dumb Object, 3 km wide.
  - [critic] ANN vols 2-4 compares it to Made in Abyss, inverted.
  - [critic] Manga Book Club still sees "impossible scale" and compares it to Sidonia and the City.
  - [fan] ResetEra: "No one does scale like Nihei."
- **Counted and mapped.**
  - [fan, inferred] Floor numbers, pillar coordinates, a cross-section map and about 50,000 steps make the tower a gridded dungeon. BLAME!'s City has no such index. No reviewer states this contrast.
- **Tone.**
  - [fan] ResetEra post #2: far less grim and oppressive than BLAME!, "almost lighthearted".
  - [critic] Manga Book Club: a more hopeful tone than BLAME!'s "overwhelming sense of impending doom".
- **Dialogue and readability.**
  - [critic] inkstone: BLAME!'s early appeal was near-wordless groping through the world. Tower Dungeon has the avant-garde edge toned down and is general-audience and readable.
  - [critic] shinentai calls it a fusion of BLAME!'s megastructure space sense and Sidonia's character relationships.
- **Expectation mismatch.** A Booklive reader (anonymous, Feb 2024) expected to climb the tower "like BLAM", but found it was not that. Another (kam) says the picture style differs but it feels similar.
- **Creatures.** [critic] ANN: "In place of biomechanical monstrosities", the monsters are "gooey and organic".
- **Detail density.** [critic] AIPT and Goodreads (Mancini, Nel): less hyper-detailed, "didn't punch as much".
- **Shared devices.**
  - [critic] Goodreads (Shells): remnants of BLAME!'s landscapes.
  - [fan] The Safeguard and Toha marks recur.
  - [critic] AIPT says double-page spreads were BLAME!'s world-building tool.

## 6. Source list notes

- The strongest architectural descriptions are in ANN (vols 1 and 2-4), Urbane Turtle, Anime Atelier, AIPT, Manga Book Club and shangrila69.
- Floor-by-floor detail below floor 14 comes almost entirely from the wiki, because reviewers rarely give floor numbers beyond 50, 59 and 80.

## Transferable design levers (as listed by the agent)

- Spatial identity by one or two sentence-level traits per floor: floor 1 columned ruin, floor 14 looping identical rooms with a waterway, floor 50 columned hall with a bridge and a gatekeeper stair, floor 59 Great Hall, floor 80 tower-wide hall. Reviewers remember halls, not corridors.
- Floor-number pillars with coordinates (wiki-attested) as a diegetic HUD: the player reads which floor they are on from a pillar sprite in the scene rather than from a counter.
- One reveal-scale hall: a floor 80 room that is as wide as the whole tower, shown as a cross-section silhouette, with the dragon and the far stair as the only landmarks. Pair it with the horizon-line idea, which is inferred, not attested.
- Stair guard as threshold: a gatekeeper standing at a stair or door is the repeatable gate unit. Royal-blood locks can be the one door type gatekeepers override.
- Crumbling spiral stairs and narrow flights with sheer drops: render stairs as the dizzying vertical set piece (overhead or low angles), the one place the camera is allowed to distort.
- Walls that break: ledges and walls giving way under the strong hero, opening shortcuts and crawlspaces (the Ruins-Dwellers warren). A destructible-wall rule gives discovery without a new system.
- Exterior openings: wooden stairs and walkways on the outer wall, open-air passages and the entrance bridge, so the tower has a visible outside against the sky.
- A small hidden shortcut line from floor 1 to floor 80 whose visual identity is a spiral staircase, repaired late (the top section is damaged at first). The repair is a natural unlock moment.
- Loop floor: identical numbered rooms that trap the party, solved by changing route (break a wall, take another door). Cheap to build from one room tile.
- Wordless landscape beats: long silent stretches and single large landscape panels between fights, with the tower silhouette against cloud.
- Vault and column vocabulary: stone vaults, massive columns, crumbling masonry, with brick, stone and wood only and no metal machinery, so the 'white Nihei' reading comes from line cleanliness, not from detail.
- Contrast rule: simple clean outline for architecture, heavy black fill for interiors and cavities, with pale creatures and armour masks reading as white shapes against the ink. This is the reviewer-attested combination.
- Tower seen from outside as a floating pillar that dwarfs mountains and ends in cloud, shown at menu or map scale before entering.
- Pillar maze as a named floor type: a field of identical pillars that act as walls, identified by their floor-number faces. Ch.28 is title-only, so the layout is a free design choice.
- Legibility guard: reviewers repeatedly say panels are hard to parse. Keep silhouettes distinct and avoid dense hatching so 2.5D sprites read at small size.

## Unverified items (as listed by the agent)

- Floor 80 'horizon line': no source uses it. The wiki says only that the hall 'stretches horizontally like a cross-section of the whole tower'.
- Floor 80 dimensions (1.86 miles by 219 yards) are wiki-only and conflict with the 10 m per floor figure.
- The 12 main pillars, 36 sections, ring dividers and 50,000 steps are wiki-only. No critic describes them. The mancome comment only confirms a map scene with 18 km, 3 km and 1800.
- Wooden stairs on the outer wall: wiki ch.2 summary only.
- Floor 14 identical looping rooms and the waterway: wiki ch.3 summary only.
- 'Inside larger than outside': one blog (Manga Book Club). Wikipedia's 'impossible geometry' is uncited. The mintotaku blog echoes ANN, so it is not independent.
- 'Organic growth' of the building itself: not supported. Wikipedia attributes 'organic architecture' to ANN's Cormack, but I did not find the phrase in the ANN text I could read.
- Ch.28 pillar maze content: nothing beyond the official title 柱の大迷宮と三角の進軍. The wiki's gloss concerns Funapa's gathering trait.
- Look of the hidden spiral staircase (width, whether central, how it joins floors): no review describes it. Its existence is attested.
- Floor 100 well and floor 11 details: wiki one-liners only.
