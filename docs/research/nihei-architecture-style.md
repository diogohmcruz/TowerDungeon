# Nihei's architectural style

> **Status: unverified first-pass research (2026-10-10).** This report came from a single research agent and did **not** get an independent skeptical verification pass, unlike the other files in this folder. Its quotations, biographical claims about Tsutomu Nihei and influence claims were **not** independently checked, and the design document that used it (`docs/brainstorm/architecture-and-visual-identity.md`) deliberately treats claims that rest only on this report as `[U]`, i.e. as [Invented] for every decision. # Nihei's architectural sensibility: what is known, what is argued

Labels: [primary] = Nihei's own words or his production material. [critic] = named critic, academic or reviewer. [fan] = blog, wiki or social post. [inferred] = my reading, not a source claim.

Short version: the architecture-trained-author story is true in a modest form. Nihei worked in construction and drafting. The "architect" label is his own to refuse: he said he "wouldn't go so far as to say I worked as an architect." Most of the lofty comparisons (Piranesi, Metabolism, Kowloon) come from critics or fans, not from him. His own stated devices are narrower and more useful for a game: structure first, function-driven design, verticality, no assistants, and a deliberate move from black to line-and-white.

## 1. Training and background (verified as far as sources allow)

- **Construction and drafting, not a degree.** Japanese Wikipedia says he graduated from Fukushima Prefectural Koriyama Kita Technical High School, moved to Tokyo, and took construction jobs including trainee site supervisor and drafting shop drawings (施工図の製図). He found he was unsuited to working inside an organisation, left, and went to the US to study drawing [[ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%BC%90%E7%93%B6%E5%8B%89)] [fan/reference; it cites Fukushima Minyu and BookWalker/BookLive Q&As]. An aggregator says "architecture-focused high school" [[manga-tantei](https://manga-tantei.com/niheitsutomu-28921)] [fan].
- **In his own words (2016 Evening interview, translated):** after high school he worked for a construction company; watching "buildings get built, the heavy machinery coming in and digging out those giant spaces… the sheer scale of all that must've influenced me in some vague way". Later he worked part-time at an architectural design firm and learned "how the insides of walls are structured" and that buildings over ten storeys "are made with steel framing inside", so he can "draw that stuff without having to look at anything for reference" [[manga brog translation](https://mangabrog.wordpress.com/2016/02/29/a-2016-interview-with-tsutomu-nihei/)] [primary, via a fan translation. The original Evening text was not seen].
- **He disclaims the title.** Asked about construction and architecture in the Anime News Network (ANN) interview: "I wouldn't go so far as to say I worked as an architect!", while agreeing "That influence is there." [[ANN interview](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interview/2016-08-26/knights-of-sidonia-mangaka-tsutomu-nihei/.105800)] [primary].
- **Passion, no single master.** To Planète BD: "I'm passionate about architecture, but no particular architect or specific style really influenced me" [[Planète BD, Demay, interview dated 25 Jan 2019](https://www.planetebd.com/interview/tsutomu-nihei/1274.html)] [primary, my translation from the French]. To Glénat: "I think my works reflect my love of architectural structures" [[Glénat](https://www.glenat.com/actualite/interview-de-tsutomu-nihei/)] [primary; the "Architecte de formation" tag is the editor's].
- **New York / Parsons.** Wikipedia and several sites say he went to New York and attended Parsons for about 11 months [[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutomu_Nihei)] [fan/reference, citing ANN]. The ANN piece says he spent about a year trying to debut in Japan before going to New York, while Japanese Wikipedia puts the US stay first, so the order is unsettled. Treat Parsons as [unverified detail].
- **Path into manga.** 1995 Afternoon Four Seasons Award special prize (judge Jiro Taniguchi) for the one-shot BLAME; about five months as assistant to Tsutomu Takahashi (in his words it "gave me the technical know-how I lacked"); BLAME! serial from 1997 [ja.wikipedia; manga brog].
- **Claims to discard.** "Studied at Tokyo University" (a search-engine summary), "studied architecture at university" (a bookshop blurb) and "trained as an architect" (Yokogao, no source) contradict or outrun the primary statements.
- **There is a 2006 interview with studio visit, "人も建築も、世界に溶け込んだひとつの要素として" ("People and buildings alike, as elements dissolved into the world").** It is in 美術手帖 (Bijutsu Techo) vol. 58 no. 876, Feb 2006, pp. 30-35 [[CiNii record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1523951030903043968), bibliographic data only; issue details from a search summary]. Its text was not readable. The title alone is a strong statement of his stance (see 3.2), but I cannot cite the contents.

## 2. Influences: what he actually says versus what critics add

- **Giger [primary].** "He influenced me enormously, especially for creature and monster designs. I don't think I'll ever shake off Giger's influence." He adds that Giger "drew many buildings and gigantic volumes using a lot of perspective" [Planète BD]. So Giger matters for both the biomechanical surface and the use of perspective on huge volumes.
- **Piranesi [critic, not Nihei].** The interviewer showed him the *Imaginary Prisons*; Nihei said he did not know them but liked them [Planète BD] [primary]. Piranesi comparisons exist in blogs and a TikTok caption [fan] and a Japanese note.com essay [fan]. Safe wording: a convergent resemblance, not an influence.
- **Other named influences [primary]:** AKIRA, Nausicaä, Ghost in the Shell (BookWalker Q&A, [BookWalker feature](https://bookwalker.jp/ex/feature/niheiworld/)); Moebius, "paintings, films, landscapes I see in the street" (Glénat); Clive Barker, Metallica, Blade Runner as favourite SF film (Planète BD); *The Thing* for the tentacled Gauna, and AKIRA and Nausicaä because they contain only props that fit their worlds [[BookLive 2014](https://booklive.jp/feature/index/id/wagakoma1406), partly paywalled]; William Gibson's settings ("I'm always trying to do something similar") and sci-fi novels as the source of most ideas [ANN; manga brog]. Wizardry and gamebooks as a reading model (2007 PUBLIC-IMAGE.ORG interview, quoted at [Technically Impossible](https://impsbl.hatenablog.jp/entry/NiheiInterview)). Also Aldiss's *Hothouse* (地球の長い午後), per a search summary of an interview that I could not open [unverified].
- **Metabolism [critic, weakly sourced].** A journal article calls Metabolism "Nihei's chief theoretical reference" and reads BLAME! as distorted Metabolism, Sidonia as an arcology, and Biomega's Restorative as Metabolist hybridity [[Speculative Architectures in Comics, Finfar](http://journal.finfar.org/articles/speculative-architectures-in-comics/)]. It gives no Nihei quote for this, and it cites Ohsawa, De Domenico and Johnson secondhand. A 2021 architecture thesis on BLAME! never mentions Metabolism [[Ferron, Laurentian](https://laurentian.scholaris.ca/items/cbde7d51-2460-4da0-bfdb-99e5b762b8f1)]. Treat as an interpretive lens, not documented influence.
- **Kowloon Walled City, Metropolis, Archigram.** No source ties Nihei to any of them [searched; negative result]. Kowloon, Archigram and Metropolis appear only in fan captions and unrelated essays.

## 3. Recurring devices (each with its evidence)

### 3.1 Megastructure scale and the dwarfed figure
- BLAME!'s City is a megastructure "measured in light years" [[Yokogao](https://www.yokogaomag.com/editorial/infinite-world-of-blame-manga)] [critic]. A Jupiter-sized room shows Killy as a tiny figure in a corner with no text (BLAME! vol. 9) [Finfar] [critic, with volume citation].
- A Substack reading: "scale is felt through ruins" and a small Kyrii in huge frames, with captions pointing to him ("Bottom right corner on the bridge") [[Zahr](https://zahr.substack.com/p/blame-the-dying-earth-masterpiece)] [fan/critic]. The "city seen through an ant's perspective" line is unsourced [fan, unverified].
- Nihei: of BLAME! "I was trying to see how small I could draw things" [ANN] [primary]. The scale is partly a drawing-detail ambition, not only spectacle.
- Nihei says he starts from the environment, for example a spaceship interior, and develops characters afterwards [manga brog] [primary].
- A critic notes the contrast: Schuiten and Peeters' cities oppress humans, while Nihei explores a wider range of building-person relationships [Finfar] [critic]. The 2006 interview title fits that reading [inferred].

### 3.2 Silence and structure as storyteller
- Little dialogue; story assembled "from urban details, hidden symbols, and the gaps between panels" [[Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/influence-blame-videogame-architecture-rising/)] [critic]. Finfar classifies the two-page silent view of the capital as a "background" panel and silent panel runs as "elaboration" (citing Bateman) [critic].
- Nihei wanted BLAME! to be something "readers could actively interpret", like a gamebook (2007 interview) [primary]. He wanted BIOMEGA to be the opposite, an easy read [primary].

### 3.3 Verticality and impossible depth
- Director Seshita on the film: "we design scenes to emphasise vertical space wherever the characters are... Sidonia also had up-and-down spaces, a hallmark of Nihei's works" [[Anime Da Vinci](https://ddnavi.com/interview/376299/a/)] [primary for the director's statement; Nihei's own spatial comments are absent there].
- Nihei drew the film's village as "a structure long vertically" and even cross-sections "to convey the structure" [[Quarterly S interview](https://www.s-ss-s.com/c/movie-blame-interview)] [primary].
- Anime Atelier on Tower Dungeon vol 1: a three-page reveal of the Dragon Tower's size, "overhead shots and puzzling angles", "narrow staircases and sheer drops" for claustrophobia, and "excellent perspective work" [[Anime Atelier](https://www.anime-atelier.com/review-tower-dungeon-vol-1/)] [critic]. ANN: "bizarre yet effective angles that truly conjure an air of terrifying verticality" and a tower described as a "massive pillar of indeterminate size, levitating vertically, high above the mountains it dwarfs" [[ANN review](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/tower-dungeon/volume-1/.226924)] [critic].

### 3.4 Function over decoration; brutalist and structural surfaces
- Nihei: "I pursue functionality", with "as few surfaces as possible", the ideal being "a natural, rational form that no human-designed object has" (whale skeleton as his example) [Quarterly S] [primary]. That was said of the film's creature and armour design, not of buildings, but it shows the principle [inferred as architecture-relevant].
- He weighed leaving structure exposed versus covering it in plaster-like cladding and drew both versions for the village [Quarterly S] [primary]. He drew setting art with loose, wobbly lines, even for things meant to be straight [Quarterly S].
- He praised the film's pipe-framed shots ("shooting people through pipes") [Quarterly S] [primary].
- Critics: "endless concrete caverns, cables, and staircases that lead nowhere" [Yokogao]; Tower Dungeon "buildings look like they've been hewn straight from rock, or hastily cobbled together with ill-fitting blocks" and "organic architecture" [ANN review] [critic].

### 3.5 Architecture versus an indifferent system
- BLAME!'s City is a machine-built, unchecked growth. The Kill Screen essay reads it as an urban organism that "doesn't seem to serve humanity" [critic] and the Ferron thesis reads it as a critique of expansion-driven development, listing the "absence of scale, orientation, time, and human intervention" as central [critic]. Finfar claims Nihei said he wanted to explore development with no control becoming anarchic, via Ohsawa, secondhand [critic, unverified against Nihei].
- Counter-evidence: asked about social commentary in BIOMEGA, he said "not even a little (laughs)" [2007 interview] [primary]. Do not overclaim authorial intent.

### 3.6 Black Nihei versus white Nihei
- I did not find "black Nihei / white Nihei" as an established critical term. It appears as reviewer shorthand in the repo's own notes [fan]. The underlying fact is [primary]: he "really liked using a lot of black", found painting black backgrounds "really tough", said heavy black "limits detail", and now prefers line: "when there's more linework than dark blacks, I'm able to convey more information", adding "Quit painting everything black as soon as possible!" [ANN]. To Planète BD: people assume white pages mean less work, "it's not true at all", and lines can take longer than filling pages with black. To Glénat: formerly generous use of tones and solid black, now "expressing myself only through line". Reasons given: working alone makes style changes easy, and Sidonia was meant to be more accessible, with "Tezuka-level mass appeal".
- ANN's Tower Dungeon review notes earlier works had a "colder aesthetic", and the current style is simpler, "evocative of Tatsuki Fujimoto" [critic].

## 4. How he draws backgrounds

- **No assistants for backgrounds, by choice.** "A background drawn by someone else just doesn't look right"; "I just want to draw it all by myself" [manga brog] [primary]. Ja Wikipedia says the same [reference].
- **Tools.** Copic multiliner and brush pen at the time of ABARA; colour in acrylic early, then Photoshop from mid-BLAME!; he builds physical models of mechs for Biomega and Sidonia as drawing reference [ja.wikipedia] [reference, not verified against Nihei]. Early submissions were pen on paper [BookLive 2014] [primary].
- **Drawing from memory.** Steel framing, wall interiors: "I can draw that without reference" [manga brog] [primary]. A Namu wiki claim that he draws without rough sketches is [fan, unverified]. He says many ideas come from doodling [manga brog].
- **3D.** The manga is drawn by hand/digital 2D; I found no source saying he uses 3D for manga backgrounds [unverified]. For the film he drew weekly design sheets over about five months at Polygon Pictures, "like souvenirs", plus exterior views and structure drawings [[Anime!Anime!](https://animeanime.jp/article/2017/05/20/33930_3.html); [Kodansha product description of the settings book was blocked (403)]]. He said 3D sometimes departs from the image in his head, and some designs became "too heavy to move" [Quarterly S] [primary].
- **Sidonia's frontispieces.** Each chapter opens with a "100 Views of Sidonia" illustration of the artificial city [Finfar; Japanese reader sources] [critic]. Nihei on setting: Japanese scenery is "easier to draw because it's a culture I'm familiar with"; invented in-world vocabulary he loves [BookLive 2014] [primary].

## 5. Per-work architectural identity

- **BLAME! (1997-2003):** the megastructure as protagonist; black-heavy; wordless; extreme vertical shafts; the human as eyes only.
- **NOiSE, ABARA, BIOMEGA:** same visual grammar at smaller or faster scale. Biomega's Restorative is a cylinder from the Moon's orbit to Neptune with continent-sized sections (vol. 5) [Finfar, citing the volume]. ABARA is rough and sketch-like, which a reviewer says adds to the dystopian atmosphere [fan]. I found no architecture-specific primary source for NOiSE, ABARA or Blame Academy [gap; Blame Academy (BLAME! Gakuen) is a parody and I found nothing architectural].
- **Knights of Sidonia (2009-2015):** an arcology; the main tower holds living space, engine and rail-gun, housing modelled on Japanese culture, 29 km long [Finfar, partly uncited] [critic]. He said the cozy living spaces were "not really" intentional [ANN].
- **Tower Dungeon (2023-):** a floating ancient tower in a fantasy genre. I found no Nihei interview about its design [searched; Kodansha pages and Natalie are blocked or silent]. Everything on its architecture is [critic] (ANN, Anime Atelier, Anime UK News "more visually focused than character-driven"). The repo's own notes record 12 full-height pillars, per-floor reference pillars, about 50,000 steps and a 3 km width (`docs/research/tower-dungeon-since-vol6.md`, section 4). I did not independently re-verify those.

## Transferable design levers (as listed by the agent)

- Structure-first worldbuilding: design the tower from its structure and function (pillars, shafts, load paths) and let story and enemies sit inside it, matching Nihei's habit of starting from the environment and developing characters afterwards. Keep the 12 full-height pillars as the readable skeleton of every floor.
- Tiny player, vast space: render the player character very small against the structure, use wide static or slow-pan camera framing, and keep long no-event traversals so scale is felt through time in transit (Kill Screen and Zahr readings).
- Pillar, girder and grid rhythm: repeat one module (pillar, beam span, bridge) along a floor with deliberate breaks, so the rhythm is the visual identity and breaks signal places of interest or danger. Per-floor reference pillars already fit this.
- Vertical shafts as the signature view: the camera or a viewpoint beat looking straight down or up a central shaft, with stairs and bridges crossing it, to deliver the 'impossible depth' shot (Seshita's 'vertical emphasis', ANN's 'terrifying verticality').
- Staircases and bridges as the main verbs: narrow stairs, sheer drops, drop-away bridges (Seshita's film village used a very steep single stair and droppable bridges) as combat and navigation geometry, not decoration.
- Overhead and skewed angles: allow rotating or tilted views that show the stack of floors below, rather than keeping the 2.5D camera at one comfortable angle.
- Cross-section reveal: after a milestone (floor 80 staircase, a ride up) show a cut-away of the tower with the player's position, echoing Nihei's habit of drawing cross-sections to convey structure. Doubles as a map and progress tool.
- Two palettes, one world: treat 'black' and 'white' as two zone moods, not eras. Clean white or light-grey stone with deep black voids and shadows for the main tower; fully black, high-density, tone-heavy zones for the necromancer or deep floors. Contrast is carried by line and silhouette, not by many colours.
- Negative space as architecture: leave large unlit or empty areas and let a few thin, precisely drawn lines (rail, stair edge, pillar) imply the rest; this is cheaper to build and matches his line-over-black preference.
- Brutalist block surfaces: monolithic hewn-rock and ill-fitting block walls, exposed structure rather than cladding, as few separate surfaces as possible. Cheap to render and consistent with the tower's 'cobbled' look.
- Silence as a design rule: minimal dialogue and text, environment tells the story (reference pillars, ruined rooms, remains, in-world symbols). Reserve music and sound for thresholds; use ambient hum, wind in shafts and distant footsteps for scale.
- Indifferent system, not villain monologue: let gatekeepers, locked doors and looping identical rooms (floors 14-15) read as the tower's own rules, with the necromancer's intrusions visibly foreign to its logic.
- Pipe and conduit framing: shoot or place characters through pipes, grates and lattices to create foreground layering (a shot Nihei praised in the film).
- Handmade-line outlines: if shaders are used, prefer a hand-drawn line pass with slightly wobbly lines (his setting drawings use loose lines for things that should be straight) rather than perfect CAD edges.
- Floor-to-floor typology: make each floor a different spatial type (columned ruin, vertical shaft hall, flooded gallery, great hall) so the tower reads as accumulated, unplanned construction, consistent with 'organic architecture' reviews and with Nihei's note that BLAME's growth is uncontrolled.
