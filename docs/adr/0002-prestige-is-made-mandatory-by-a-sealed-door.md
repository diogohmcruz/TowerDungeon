# Prestige is made mandatory by a sealed Door below the summit

The party can clear the floor beneath the summit but cannot go higher until the roll holds the inheritance that opens the Door, which costs more legacy than a first campaign earns. So at least one prestige is required to reach Victory. A voluntary Exodus is a third campaign ending beside Victory and Defeat, because Defeat is almost unreachable in the current rules and a stalled player would otherwise have no way out. Full design: `docs/brainstorm/prestige-and-persistence.md`, sections 3.2 and 3.7.

Tower Wizard's prestige was criticised as avoidable ("winnable without it"), linear and samey. An optional prestige would repeat that, and a bigger tower-memory penalty alone does not force the player to spend legacy. A sealed Door does, while keeping the first campaign a complete game with a foreshadowed ending.

## Consequences

- A player who never reaches the Door can ignore prestige indefinitely, and also never sees an ending.
- The Door's position follows the tower's height. If the enemy and supplies scaling pass changes it, the Door moves with it.
- Standing at the Door costs lives (attrition and supplies drain continue), so the Door check must pay the floor's loot only once.
- The first prestige loop alone is "samey" (the same tower, only harder). Variation (mutations, omens, pacts) is planned as the answer, and it should be playtested before it is built.
